# Security review handoff

This is the implementation contributor's review aid, **not an independent audit**. The workflow's independent reviewer must inspect the accepted source and generated manifest before deployment. Local tests and lint warnings do not substitute for that review. Slither/Mythril were not run in this assignment; the task assigns independent static analysis to the verifier.

## Asset custody and authorization

`PimdMarkets` holds USDC and explicitly listed collateral assets. Free balances, order escrow, and market collateral are separate accounting buckets. A market's complete outcome supply is backed by one USDC plus its fixed asset units per pair before settlement. Execution's single rounded cash amount is used for both counterparties. Share-to-asset conversions are exact because the registry requires units divisible by the share scale. Splits/merges and winning/refund claims debit reserves before any withdrawal.

`BoundaryOracle` holds separate IMD bonds and refund/reward credits. It cannot withdraw market collateral. Oracle fees are initially advanced outside the two bonds so a failure can return both bonds in full. On successful resolution, the fee advance is reimbursed from the losing bond. The request subcall is callable only by the oracle itself and is entered from a reentrancy-guarded dispute/retry; two paid requests either both succeed or both roll back. An unavailable or unaffordable service cannot turn an already disputed price back into an unchallenged price.

The registry's immutable explicit owner can append asset versions and periods. It cannot rewrite a pinned version or market. The market's immutable explicit owner is only a fee recipient; it uses normal free-balance withdrawals. There is no pause, privileged collateral sweep, arbitrary transfer, upgrade, or initializer. No constructor touches another contract or moves the launch supply. Review manifest wiring to ensure the registry and market use the same `$owner` and all other dependencies are the delivered immutable contracts.

All token pulls use `transferFrom(msg.sender, ...)`. Deposits and swap inputs check exact balance deltas. Public balance-changing calls are guarded against reentrancy; state debits precede external withdrawals. The v4 callback separately authenticates its fixed caller and the hash of an in-flight request, and consumes that authorization once. No ETH is routed through users or external receivers; intermediate native currency deltas net within PoolManager. Approvals are exact and zeroed after use.

## Ordering, rounding, and liveness

The book contains separate sorted linked lists for bids and asks. Matching chooses each best price, FIFO inside that price, with the earlier of the selected two orders as maker. Partial fills retain IDs and priority. A capped limit order remains escrowed; `matchMarket` and later placements continue matching. The lists may therefore temporarily cross, representing pending executions. No caller receives a reward merely for continuing matching. IOC remainders are cancelled. An exhausted open-pair cap blocks additional pair creation until capacity is freed, while transfers/merges that reach the heads remain supported.

Execution rounds a cash amount up by less than one USDC atomic unit. A 1024-atomic-unit reserve covers this over at most 1024 fills for an order. Fees round up in their payment coin, while rebates round down. Those charges are deducted only from the user's explicit locked fee budget, which at placement must cover the order's own execution at its limit price under the latest boundary price, so free unfillable orders cannot occupy priority or takers' steps. Old orders whose fee budgets or short-swap budgets no longer suffice can be cancelled during matching; the newly submitted taker's failed swap/fee payment reverts its whole transaction. Cancellation refunds remaining collateral, swap budget and fees, including after end/settlement. These behaviors need frontend disclosure.

Every bounded loop has a finite limit: 64 matching/expiry/batch steps, 128 automatic insertion hops, 32 periods, 3 route hops, and at most 65 MPT proof nodes/steps. Users can provide validated insertion predecessors. Gas limits can require smaller sweeps or separate proof transactions; they never authorize spending another user's free balance. Expiry and settlement are permissionless transactions, not automatic wall-clock mutations.

## Historical proof review

The repository includes complete strict RLP and secure Merkle Patricia inclusion libraries. These are original implementation code, not a claim of a prior third-party audit. Review canonical short/long lengths, integer leading zeros, branch/extension/leaf paths, odd/even compact-path flags, inline versus hashed children, exact proof consumption, and the secure-key double hashing. Tests replay real Ethereum state proofs, reject corruption and extra/missing nodes, and validate the real history anchor on a mainnet fork. Further independent differential testing of adversarial MPT encodings is appropriate before release.

The newest header's hash must be available from EIP-2935. Consecutive block numbers and parent hashes authenticate the two earlier headers. All account and slot proofs are rooted in these headers. Slot0's lower 160 bits supply sqrtPriceX96; each path direction follows the configured token. Price arithmetic handles both 192- and 128-bit squared-ratio regimes with full-precision multiplication/division. The median is taken after computing each block's complete path price. Prices must be positive and at most `10^30` in 18-decimal USD.

The 27-hour proof deadline is independent of EIP-2935's exact 8191-block range. Reorganizations change canonical headers and transactions together; clients should still wait for confirmations and roll back indexed logs on reorg. The bundled proof builder detects header-hash mismatches/reorganizations. Future header fields may require an updated offchain builder; the contract permits appended header fields while requiring the authenticated legacy positions.

## Economic and external trust assumptions

1. **Spot-price manipulation remains possible.** The requested three-block median is not a long TWAP. It resists a one-sample outlier, but an attacker able to affect two samples, persistently move an illiquid pool, or influence consecutive blocks can affect settlement. This mechanism was kept as requested. Independent reviewers should compare market caps and collateral at risk against pool depth and manipulation costs.
2. **Optimistic finality needs observers.** An incorrect 10-IMD proposal becomes final if nobody challenges within one hour. The bond is fixed by the brief and can be small relative to a market. Permissionless operation removes a designated keeper; it does not guarantee somebody will submit a proof or dispute a proposal. The contract cannot verify that the proposed block window straddles the boundary; the panel refuses a false window, which ends in a refund of both bonds. A refunded proposal frees its slot, so a false proposal cannot lock a boundary into a void, but each refused round costs its challenger one oracle fee and part of the proposal window. The brief's "oracle failure returns both bonds" rule is kept; a forfeit of the proposer's bond on a refused window would be the stronger deterrent and is a policy choice for the requester.
3. **The fallback's statistical convention differs.** Two independent `univ4-spot` uint256 questions yield each pool's median quote. Their composition need not equal the median of three complete multi-pool prices. The source and README make this difference explicit. The independent reviewer should treat this as a concrete interpretation of the two-question fallback requirement and confirm it against the approved settlement intent before release.
4. **The fixed oracle service is trusted on its fallback answers.** Signatures authenticate an attestation, not the honesty of its panel or signer. The Intake writer cannot forge a signed answer but can withhold callbacks or signal failure. The contract requires its own pending Intake request, a canonical signed question hash, exact chain/window, answer type, panel/quorum bounds, and nonexpired EIP-712 authorization. Oracle request UUIDs and Intake request IDs are different namespaces. Signed IDs cannot be replayed after use.
5. **The signer and Intake cannot rotate.** They are the brief's fixed addresses. Disputed boundaries may remain unpriced if that service retires or fails. Deferred request attempts can be retried; failed/silent resolutions refund bonds, free the slot for a new proposal while the window lasts, and missing market prices eventually cause refunds. A challenger can delay fallback settlement through a dispute, and timeout/refund is intentionally a possible result. A normal market whose start price can no longer be recorded stops accepting orders, pairs and executions (`priceable`), so a certain void cannot be traded against uninformed counterparties; its existing positions settle as a refund.
6. **USDC and future listed tokens have issuer risk.** Issuer freezes can block transfers. Rebasing, transfer-tax, callback-heavy or malicious tokens are unsuitable listings even though the owner can identify an ERC-20 as an asset. An owner controls future-market economics through units, caps and paths, but cannot alter old snapshots. The initial IMD path is enforced exactly. Future routes permit the factory's initialization-only guard but reject other hook permission bits, so swaps have no hook callbacks.
7. **Fees use recorded boundaries.** An old latest boundary can make fee conversion stale. Users should inspect it and bound fees/swaps; the contract does not replace it with a manipulable intratransaction quote. Public mempool swaps can be sandwiched within a user's maximum; exact output and maximum input prevent exceeding that consent, not all adverse execution.
8. **Immutable deployment requires correct wiring.** The launch service must use the six documented artifacts and dependency order. No application receives a launch token allocation. Policy controls token distribution, pool fees, factory guard/distributor, source publication, attestation and admission; these are outside application source authority.

## Validation scope

The default tests cover token supply/transfers, owner authorization, calendar boundaries, historical proof success/failure, order combinations, price/FIFO priority, capped continuations, expiry/cancel/refunds, rounding, fee distribution, swaps, callback authentication, oracle signature/error paths, finalization/void/claims, deployment size/opcodes, and stateful conservation. Mainnet fork tests validate actual token semantics, pool path, delta directions, native intermediary netting, input bounds, and account/storage proof interpretation. Oracle signing tests use an explicitly identified test ERC-1271 signer; they do not claim to have purchased or received a live oracle answer. The published canonical-question hash format was also cross-checked against a public attested request.

Outstanding workflow work is an **independent** adversarial review of source plus manifest and the services' publication/deployment checks. No transaction was broadcast, no funded wallet was accessed, and no independent approval is claimed here.
